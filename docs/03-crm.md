# CRM: remember people without keeping a brain full of sticky notes

**CRM** stands for customer relationship management. In a tiny business, it mostly means: **know who you talked to, what you promised, and what happens next.** You do not need an enterprise platform to do that.

> **Not legal advice.** Privacy, marketing consent, record retention, and data protection rules vary. Collect less personal information, protect what you collect, and ask a qualified professional when a customer relationship involves sensitive or regulated data.

## The free starting point

### Simple and free forever: a spreadsheet

For your first 20–50 relationships, a spreadsheet can be the right CRM. Use columns such as:

- Name
- Preferred name and pronouns (optional, for respectful communication)
- Email / preferred contact method
- How they found you
- What they're interested in
- Last conversation date
- What I promised
- What they promised
- Next action and due date
- Consent / source of contact
- Notes (no sensitive data)

This is boring, exportable, and easy to understand. The weakness is that it needs manual updating and doesn't remind you by itself.

### When you need a pipeline: HubSpot Free

[HubSpot's free CRM](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/crm) stores up to **1,000 contacts** for free, with no time limit, and includes core contact and deal management. It is a good next step when you have more conversations than your memory can carry or you want reminders, pipelines, and a shared view of customer activity.

Why it can be a good beginner choice:

- Free contact and deal management.
- A real interface for follow-ups rather than an infinite spreadsheet.
- You can start with no paid onboarding.
- It can grow with you if the business someday needs more.

Why it is not automatically the answer:

- It's a large product with many upsells. A tiny business can get lost in modules it doesn't need.
- Free-plan limits and features change.
- It stores customer data in a third-party cloud. You need a reason, permission, and a security habit.
- HubSpot's broader marketing and sales products can become expensive. The free CRM does not mean the whole platform is free.

**Start with the simplest tool that makes you follow through.** If a spreadsheet works, don't install a CRM to feel professional.

## Build a one-person pipeline that stays small

You need fewer stages than the internet suggests. Try four:

1. **New** — someone made contact.
2. **Talking** — a real conversation is happening.
3. **Proposal / next step** — you've offered something or agreed on a next action.
4. **Won or lost** — with a one-line reason.

Then add a **next action and due date to every open contact**. A pipeline with no next actions is a museum.

### The 15-minute weekly relationship review

Once a week, open the CRM (or spreadsheet) and do this:

- Which conversations have no next action? Add one.
- Who is waiting on me? Send the thing.
- Who am I waiting on? Send one gentle nudge.
- Who is a good fit for what I offer now? Send a useful, non-spammy note.
- Update "won/lost" so the pipeline reflects reality.
- Archive the people who asked not to be contacted.

## Get consent right, keep data minimal

These are practical guardrails, not legal advice:

- **Record where the contact came from.** "Bought a list" is not a good starting point for permission-based outreach.
- **Don't buy email lists.** They damage deliverability and create consent problems.
- **Make unsubscribing or asking not to be contacted easy.** Honor requests promptly.
- **Don't collect sensitive data just because you have a notes field.** No Social Security numbers, health diagnoses, passwords, or bank credentials.
- **Respect health privacy especially.** If you work with healthcare audiences, do not place patient information in an ordinary CRM. HIPAA and related obligations are specialized; get professional guidance.
- **Limit access.** Enable two-factor authentication. Don't share a login on a sticky note.
- **Export quarterly.** A contact list you can't export is not fully yours.

## Set up HubSpot Free without drowning

1. Create an account with your business email.
2. Explore **Contacts** and **Deals** only. Ignore other navigation for now.
3. Create a four-stage pipeline: New, Talking, Next step, Won/Lost.
4. Add five real contacts that you have permission to store. Skip sensitive notes.
5. Add a next action and due date for each open contact.
6. Connect your business email only if you understand what data the tool will access.
7. Create a recurring 15-minute weekly review.
8. Test export and data deletion before you rely on it.

If the idea of setting up a "pipeline" makes you sigh, use the spreadsheet. Seriously. A functioning low-tech system beats an abandoned high-tech one.

## Free-plan watch-outs

- **"Free CRM" does not mean free marketing automation.** Check the boundaries before uploading your list.
- **Contact limits count contacts, not just customers.** Know what happens at the cap and whether duplicates count.
- **Email tracking has privacy implications.** Recipients and regulators may care about tracking pixels and consent. Don't enable every tracking feature by reflex.
- **The CRM is not your email marketing platform.** Keep sales conversations and consented newsletters in the appropriate tool.
- **No free plan owes you indefinite storage.** Back up.
- **Do not treat AI-generated summaries as facts.** A confident summary can still be wrong. Check the record.

## AI helper: turn notes into a follow-up plan

```text
You are a careful relationship assistant. Turn these meeting notes into a
CRM record using only facts I provide: [paste redacted notes]. Output:
1) two-sentence summary, 2) what I promised, 3) what they promised,
4) one next action with a suggested date, 5) one short, warm follow-up
email. Mark anything missing as TBD. Do not invent names, amounts, or
commitments. Do not make legal, tax, or medical recommendations.
```

**Good uses:** organize notes, draft a follow-up, prepare for a call, suggest a polite nudge.

**Bad uses:** store customer secrets in a free chatbot, infer sensitive traits, automate cold outreach, or let it write promises you haven't approved.
