# Email marketing: stay in touch without becoming a spammer

**The job:** Send useful, expected emails to people who asked to hear from you; make unsubscribing easy; keep your list portable. An email list is a borrowed audience, not a guaranteed sales funnel.

> **Not legal advice.** Marketing-email rules depend on where you and your recipients are. In the US, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guidance is a useful official starting point. If you sell to the EU/UK or handle regulated information, get jurisdiction-specific advice. Never put health records or sensitive personal data in a marketing tool.

## The free starting point

### Best for a small permission-based list: MailerLite Free

[MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/pricing) currently offers a free plan with **up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails**, plus two user seats. That's a real starting point for a one-person business, but small. You'll outgrow it before you get famous.

Good fit:

- You want a proper signup form, consent record, and unsubscribe handling.
- You have a growing but small list.
- You want campaign and automation features without a monthly bill.
- You can live within the subscriber and monthly-send limits.

### More daily sends: Brevo Free

[Brevo](https://www.brevo.com/pricing/) currently offers a free plan with **300 emails per day**, a drag-and-drop editor, templates, automation, and A/B testing. Free plans and send allowances change; check the official page for current contact and daily limits before moving a list.

Good fit:

- You send small batches frequently.
- You want to explore a combined email/CRM platform.
- You can stay under a daily sending ceiling.

**Don't run both for the same list.** Pick one. Duplicates, unsubscribes, and double sends are how trust gets lost.

## The one thing that matters most: permission

A list is not a number. It's a set of people who expect your mail. Build it with:

- **Explicit signups** from your website, a form, a workshop, or a direct request.
- **Clear expectations:** what you'll send and how often.
- **Double opt-in** if your tool supports it. It asks people to confirm by email and reduces typos and bad addresses.
- **A real unsubscribe link.** Do not hide it.
- **An honest physical mailing address** in commercial email, as required in the US. Use a business mailing address you control; don't use someone else's home without permission. Check the FTC guidance and your tool's help docs.
- **A relationship**, not a purchased file. Buying lists is a fast route to spam complaints and deliverability problems.

If someone says "stop emailing me," honor it. Don't re-add them because they downloaded a new lead magnet. Keep a do-not-contact record.

## Set up a small email system

1. **Choose one home.** Do the free-plan math for the next six months, not just today.
2. **Create a signup form.** Ask for the minimum: usually an email address and, optionally, a first name. Explain what they'll get.
3. **Set the consent language.** Keep a dated record of where and when each contact signed up. A checkbox or active opt-in is clearer than a prechecked box.
4. **Write a welcome email.** It should arrive immediately, confirm what they signed up for, set expectations, and offer one useful next step.
5. **Make one helpful monthly email.** A practical tip, a link roundup, an update on your book or services, or a question. Not five calls to action.
6. **Add a plain-text unsubscribe line.** Your provider handles the mechanism; don't bury it.
7. **Test it.** Send to yourself and a friend. Check mobile, links, images with alt text, footer address, and unsubscribe.
8. **Authenticate your domain when you own one.** Set up the records your provider recommends (often SPF, DKIM, and DMARC). This is a deliverability practice, not legal advice. Use the provider's current setup guide.
9. **Export your list quarterly.** You should know what leaving looks like.

## A calm first-month rhythm

- **Week 1:** Welcome email + one useful resource.
- **Week 2:** Short note about who you help and one thing you're working on.
- **Week 3:** Practical tip or a question you've been getting.
- **Week 4:** Review: opens are a weak signal but still a signal. What did people reply to? What caused unsubscribes? Adjust.

Metrics to watch without obsessing: delivery, replies, clicks, unsubscribes, and complaints. Open rates are distorted by privacy features and image blocking. A small list with replies is healthier than a giant list that never responds.

## CAN-SPAM guardrails in plain English

The FTC guidance describes requirements including accurate header information, truthful subject lines, clear identification of commercial messages where required, a physical postal address, and a working opt-out mechanism that is honored promptly. There are also rules about third-party sending. Read the official guide; this is not legal advice.

Also avoid:

- Fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subject lines.
- Manufactured deadlines and invented scarcity.
- Health, financial, or earnings claims you cannot support.
- Sending to people who didn't ask.
- Putting customer information or health data in the email content without a proper legal basis.

## Free-plan watch-outs

- **Subscriber counts include more than customers.** Bounced contacts and unsubscribed contacts may count differently. Check the tool.
- **Monthly sends are not the same as unlimited emails.** A big launch can hit a limit mid-month.
- **Automation features and support vary by plan.** Confirm before designing a workflow.
- **Free-plan branding may be added.** Usually acceptable; pay only when the brand cost is real.
- **Cheap does not mean compliant.** You remain responsible for consent and accurate claims.
- **Deliverability is not guaranteed** because you use a free tool. A warmed domain and relevant content matter.

## AI helper: build a welcome sequence that sounds human

```text
Help me draft a four-email welcome sequence for [describe audience]
who signed up for [lead magnet or reason]. Use plain English and short
paragraphs. No fake scarcity, no income claims, no health claims. Each
email must be under 180 words and contain one clear call to action.
Output for each: subject line, preview text, body, and one fact or link
I need to verify before sending. End with a checklist for consent,
unsubscribe, and a physical mailing address. This is marketing help,
not legal advice.
```

Then do the human part: cut the hype, verify every claim, check the footer, and send it to yourself first.
