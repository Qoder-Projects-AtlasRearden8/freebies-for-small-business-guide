# Free Tools to Run a Small Business / LLC

**A friendly, no-subscription field guide for new LLCs and one-person businesses in Indiana and the US.**

Built for beginners who need a real operating system, not a 47-tab productivity fantasy. This guide covers the jobs every small business has to do—**keep the books, send invoices, remember people, stay in touch, have a website, make things look decent, book meetings, collect answers, sign documents, find trustworthy templates, learn new skills, and get free human help**—and shows a genuinely free starting point for each one.

**Plans and links checked:** October 25, 2026. Free plans change. Verify current limits before you build a workflow around them.

> **This is education, not legal, tax, accounting, or financial advice.** No attorney-client, accountant-client, or fiduciary relationship is created. Business structures, taxes, contracts, insurance, licenses, and recordkeeping depend on facts we don't have. Check with a licensed professional in your jurisdiction before making decisions.

## Who this is for

You are probably one of these people:

- Starting a one-person LLC or sole proprietorship and trying not to spend $200 a month on software.
- Already running a tiny business and paying for tools you barely use.
- An author, creator, freelancer, consultant, reseller, or side-project owner who needs the boring parts to work.
- In Indiana and wondering what free help actually exists.

The examples use US and Indiana resources, but most of the software choices work more broadly. The guide is written in plain English, in the order you're likely to need things.

## The one rule that matters

> **Choose one home for every job, then make sure you can leave with your data.**

Tool sprawl is expensive even when the tools are free. Every new login, notification, and dashboard costs attention. The free stack in this guide is deliberately boring: one place for books, one for invoices, one for relationships, one for email, one for your site. Add complexity only when a real limit stops you.

## How to use this guide

1. **Read [Start here](00-start-here.md).** Do the tiny setup. Don't read nine chapters first.
2. **Pick your first bottleneck.** Not working invoices? Go to accounting and invoicing. No way for people to find you? Go to website. Don't install everything on day one.
3. **Follow the [90-day plan](14-90-day-plan.md).** One weekly work session is enough.
4. **Ask for help early.** Free advising from the Indiana SBDC or SCORE is not a last resort. It's free infrastructure. See [Free help](12-free-help-indiana.md).
5. **Recheck the plan pages every quarter.** Free tiers mutate. Your system should be exportable and replaceable.

## What "free" means here

The software in this guide has a **genuinely usable free tier**—not a trial, not a demo that watermarks your client work, and not a "free" plan that requires a paid add-on for the basic job. That does not mean every part of running a business is free:

- **Payment processing costs money.** Invoices are free to send; taking a card usually is not. The processor takes a percentage.
- **Domains cost money.** A `.com` usually costs roughly $10–$25 a year, though prices vary.
- **Free plans have limits.** Subscriber caps, monthly document limits, storage caps, and AI-usage caps are normal.
- **Your time is the subscription fee.** Setup and maintenance are real costs. Prefer the simpler tool.
- **Free tools can change.** A free tier can be reduced. Always know where the export button lives.
- **Certain things deserve paid help.** A CPA for a messy tax year, an attorney for a contract that matters, a bookkeeper when the backlog is real. Free tools cannot replace professional judgment.

## Safety rules for every tool

- Use a **business-only email address** and a password manager.
- Turn on **two-factor authentication**.
- Never put Social Security numbers, EINs, bank credentials, health records, or API keys in a public form, document, or AI chat.
- Don't upload other people's sensitive information to a free tool without a reason and permission.
- Export your data at least quarterly. Free plans change; your records should not be trapped.
- Check the privacy policy and data-use terms of any tool that touches customer information.
- No link from a cold email is automatically official. For Indiana filings, start at [INBiz](https://inbiz.in.gov/), not at a look-alike service.
