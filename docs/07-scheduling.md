# Scheduling: end the "what about Tuesday?" spiral

**The job:** Let people book a real meeting at a real time without exposing your whole calendar or exchanging nine emails. The best booking page is short, clear, and connected to a calendar you actually keep current.

> **Not legal advice.** If you handle appointments for healthcare or other regulated services, don't collect clinical details in a generic booking form. Use systems and policies appropriate to your obligations. This guide covers general business scheduling only.

## The free starting point

### Most flexible for one person: Cal.com Free

[Cal.com](https://cal.com/pricing) is an open-source-friendly scheduling platform with a free plan for **one user**, **unlimited event types**, calendar syncing, and integrations. It's a strong choice for a solo business that wants several meeting types—intro call, book-a-session, podcast interview, office hours—without paying per event type.

Why it works:

- One-person plan with unlimited event types.
- Connects to Google Calendar, Outlook, and other calendars.
- Good fit for local-first/self-hosting-minded users if you later want more control.
- White-labeling and larger teams are paid upgrades, not requirements for getting booked.

### Classic simple option: Calendly Free

[Calendly's free plan](https://calendly.com/pricing) includes **one event type**, one calendar connection, and a customizable booking page as of this writing. If you offer exactly one kind of meeting—say, a 20-minute discovery call—that's enough. If you need separate 15/30/60-minute meetings, Cal.com's free plan is more flexible.

**Use Google Calendar or another calendar as the source of truth.** The booking tool is the doorbell; the calendar is the house.

## Set up a booking page people can trust

1. **Choose one meeting type.** For a first version: "Intro call — 20 minutes." Write a one-sentence purpose.
2. **Connect one calendar.** Avoid connecting several personal calendars with sensitive event titles unless you understand what's shared.
3. **Set availability.** Keep it honest. Don't advertise 7 a.m.–9 p.m. if you'll resent every booking.
4. **Add buffers.** Five or ten minutes between meetings saves the day. Set a minimum notice period (for example, 24 hours) and a booking horizon (for example, the next 30 days).
5. **Check time zones.** Enable time-zone display. Confirm what the confirmation email shows. Don't book anyone at 3 a.m. by accident.
6. **Add location.** Auto-generated video link or a clearly stated phone/address. Don't put a private home address unless it's appropriate and safe.
7. **Write a short description.** What you'll discuss, what to bring, what happens next.
8. **Set cancellation and reschedule rules.** Include a link for reschedules and a gentle notice request. Let people leave without a guilt trip.
9. **Send a confirmation with a clear subject line.** "Confirmed: 20-minute intro call, Tue Oct 27, 2:00 p.m. ET."
10. **Test it yourself.** Book a slot, check the calendar event, click the video link, cancel, and reschedule.

## Keep it humane for you and them

- **Batch calls when you can.** Two or three call slots on certain days is easier than calls scattered through every day.
- **Protect focus time.** Mark unavailable blocks in your calendar. Good boundaries prevent bad meetings.
- **Add a pre-meeting form only when it helps.** A required form can create friction. Ask only what you need.
- **Don't put intake questions in the event description if they contain sensitive info.** Use a secure form and disclose retention.
- **Have a no-show lane.** Send one friendly note: "I missed you today. Want to find another time?" Then move on.
- **Accessibility:** send a link, not just a phone number; use captions when available; offer a contact method for adjustments.
- **Consent:** no adding attendees to your newsletter just because they booked a call.

## A tiny scheduling toolkit

```text
Event type: Intro call (20 min)
Purpose: Understand the project and decide the next step
Location: Video link generated by the tool
Availability: Tue/Thu, 10:00–14:00 local, with 10-minute buffers
Notice: 24 hours minimum
Reschedule: allowed up to 12 hours before
Who brings what: a short description and the one decision you want to make
```

Write this in a note and reuse it. Don't reinvent your meeting policy every week.

## Free-plan watch-outs

- **Cal.com's free plan is for one user.** Team routing and advanced branding are paid.
- **Calendly's free plan is one event type.** Multiple meeting types or advanced workflows can require a paid plan.
- **The scheduling tool is not your calendar.** If you don't maintain the calendar, you'll double-book.
- **Video conferencing may have its own limits.** Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams free options change. Check current host and participant limits.
- **Booking data lives in a third party.** Read the privacy terms and don't collect health details unnecessarily.
- **"Unlimited event types" doesn't mean unlimited everything.** Integrations, workflows, or branding may still have limits.

## AI helper: write a booking invitation that respects everyone's time

```text
Draft a short booking email for [a specific type of meeting]. My booking
page is [describe, not paste a private link]. Offer two relevant time
windows in [time zone], include a 45-minute reschedule option, and state
that the meeting is [length]. Ask for one piece of context in advance.
Keep it under 100 words and warm, not salesy. Do not claim anyone else's
availability unless I give it to you.
```

Use AI for wording, not for inventing available times. The calendar knows the truth.
