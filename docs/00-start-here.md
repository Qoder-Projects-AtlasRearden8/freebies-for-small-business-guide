# Start here: your first working version

**Goal:** In about 30 minutes, create a small, stable place for business money, business words, and business people. Not a perfect system. A working one.

> **Education, not legal or tax advice.** This chapter mentions business structure and records only as organization topics. For decisions about how your business should be formed, taxed, insured, or contracted, use a licensed attorney or CPA, or start with the free advising described in [Free help in Indiana & the US](12-free-help-indiana.md).

## The 5 things every tiny business needs first

1. **A business-only email address.** Not your personal inbox with a label. Something like `hello@yourdomain.com` later, or a separate free Gmail/Outlook account now.
2. **A business-only money trail.** A separate bank account or account you use only for the business. Business banking can have fees; ask about free accounts before applying.
3. **A books home.** For most US beginners, Wave Starter is the least expensive path because core accounting and invoicing are free.
4. **A way to be paid.** A simple invoice with clear terms, plus a payment method whose fees you understand.
5. **A way to be found or contacted.** At minimum, a one-page website and a form or email address. You don't need a brand refresh first.

Everything else is an upgrade to those five.

## The 30-minute start

### Minutes 0–8: Make a clean drawer

- Create a business-only email.
- Create three folders in a cloud drive: `Clients & customers`, `Money`, `Brand & admin`.
- Inside `Money`, create `2026` and a monthly folder. Put receipts there.
- Write one sentence describing what the business does and who it helps. Keep it in a note. This is your reusable introduction.

### Minutes 8–20: Give the money a home

- Open [Wave](https://www.waveapps.com/) and create a free Starter account.
- Add your business name and basic details.
- Connect the business bank account if you're comfortable doing so. If you're not ready, start with manual entries and add the connection later.
- Do not enter years of old transactions. Start from today, or from the beginning of the current tax year after asking a professional what you actually need.

### Minutes 20–30: Make the first request and first record

- Create one invoice template.
- Create one contact for a real or example client.
- Write down your payment terms: when payment is due, how to pay, and where to ask questions.
- Put a recurring 30-minute "Money date" on your calendar for the same day each month.

That is enough for day one.

## What to do before forming or changing an LLC

The guide is about free tools, not entity selection. But since LLC questions come up early, here are organization guardrails—not recommendations about whether or not to form an entity:

- **Indiana filings start with INBiz.** It is Indiana's official business filing portal: <https://inbiz.in.gov/>. Avoid cold calls and look-alike "LLC filing" sites that charge a service fee for something you can file directly. Confirm any fee shown in INBiz before paying.
- **An EIN is free from the IRS.** Apply directly at <https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online>. Never pay a third party just to "get an EIN" unless they're providing a service you understand and want.
- **An LLC is not a free pass on taxes or paperwork.** Annual reports, registered agent/office requirements, local licenses, taxes, and insurance can still apply. Ask the SBDC or a professional what applies to your situation.
- **Don't sign a template because it's free.** Free legal templates can be useful starting points, but a lease, business contract, operating agreement, or employment document may need an Indiana attorney's review. See [Legal-template resources](10-legal-templates.md).
- **Don't use a home address without thinking.** Public filings may expose an address. Registered-agent options and privacy trade-offs vary. Ask a professional or the Secretary of State.

## The tool map

| Job | Start with | Add later only when… |
|-----|------------|----------------------|
| Bookkeeping | Wave Starter | You need inventory, payroll, or accountant-specific workflows |
| Invoices | Zoho Invoice Free or Wave | Your volume exceeds the free invoice limit |
| Relationships | HubSpot Free or a spreadsheet | You consistently miss follow-ups |
| Email list | MailerLite Free or Brevo Free | Your list outgrows the free tier |
| Website | GitHub Pages or Netlify Free | You need a store, membership site, or complex forms |
| Design | Canva Free | You need paid brand tools or premium assets |
| Booking | Cal.com Free or Calendly Free | You need team routing and advanced workflows |
| Forms | Tally Free or Google Forms | You need paid storage or advanced permissions |
| Signatures | DocuSeal Free | You need higher volume or SMS verification |
| Help | SBDC, SCORE, SBA, IRS, INBiz | Your question genuinely needs paid expertise |

**Do not add a second tool in a category until the first one actually hurts.** Free tools are only cheap if you use them.

## Set up a backup rhythm now

Software is rented. Your records are yours.

- **Monthly:** export your accounting data as CSV and a PDF report. Download invoices.
- **Monthly:** export contacts or copy the CRM data somewhere you control.
- **Monthly:** download a copy of your email list (where the plan allows) and keep it in your private files.
- **Quarterly:** download your website source or repository and confirm you can restore it.
- **Quarterly:** check current free-plan limits and renewal dates.
- **Whenever you change tools:** export before you cancel.

Keep the backup in a private folder with access limited to you. Don't post exports to public repositories or AI chats.

## AI helper: your "set up the boring parts" prompt

Use a free chatbot, but **redact personal and financial details first**. Never paste account numbers, EINs, customer data, or documents you don't own.

```text
You are a calm operations assistant for a one-person business. Ask me
five short questions about how money, customers, and documents currently
flow through my business. Then give me a 7-step setup plan that takes no
more than 30 minutes per step. Keep the tools free, avoid asking me for
sensitive information, and label every guess as [assumption]. End with a
one-page monthly maintenance checklist. This is organizational help, not
legal, tax, or financial advice.
```

Then verify every recommendation against the tool's official pricing page.
