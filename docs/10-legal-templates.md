# Legal-template resources: start with official forms, not random PDFs

**The job:** Find trustworthy starting points for routine filings and documents, know what a template cannot do, and prepare better questions for a licensed professional.

> **This chapter is education, not legal advice and not a substitute for an attorney.** Filing a form or using a template does not create a lawyer-client relationship. Contract, employment, privacy, tax, and entity questions depend on specific facts. The sources below are official or established starting points, but you must verify that a form is current and appropriate for your situation.

## A hierarchy of trust

Use documents in this order, and only when you understand what you're filing:

1. **Official government forms and portals** for filings the government actually requires.
2. **Official guidance** that explains the filing process, deadlines, and common mistakes.
3. **Reputable educational templates** from SBA resource partners, clearly labeled as generic.
4. **Templates from commercial providers**—read the licence, terms, state-specific disclaimers, and marketing claims carefully.
5. **A document a licensed attorney drafted or reviewed for your situation**, when the stakes justify it.

A random PDF with a professional-looking header is at the bottom of that hierarchy. Free does not mean accurate or state-specific.

## The core official resources

### 1. Indiana formation, reports, and entity records

- **INBiz** is Indiana's official business filing portal: <https://inbiz.in.gov/>.
- Use it to form an entity, file entity reports, update information, and search business records.
- The Indiana Secretary of State's business resources are at <https://www.in.gov/sos/business/>.
- Indiana requires entities to maintain a registered agent and registered office in the state. Confirm current requirements in official guidance and ask a professional if you're unsure how privacy, virtual agents, or commercial registered agents interact with your situation.
- Don't pay an unfamiliar third-party "LLC filing service" from a cold call before checking INBiz and understanding what the service actually adds.

**Important:** Filing formation documents is not the same as having a complete operating agreement, licenses, permits, tax registrations, insurance, or good contracts. You may not need all of those immediately, but "I formed an LLC" is not a full legal foundation.

### 2. Federal tax and identification forms

- **EIN online:** free and official at <https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online>.
- **Form W-9:** official PDF and instructions at <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9>. You provide a W-9 to a requester who needs your taxpayer identification number; this page says not to send the form to the IRS.
- **Small business and self-employed center:** <https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed>.
- **Schedule C:** <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-c-form-1040>.
- **Self-employed tax center:** <https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center>.
- **Publication 334, Tax Guide for Small Business:** <https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334>.

Ask a CPA or enrolled agent which forms and elections apply to you. An LLC is not a tax classification by itself. Do not let an AI or a template decide your tax status.

### 3. Indiana tax and employer registrations

- **Indiana Department of Revenue, business taxes:** <https://www.in.gov/dor/i-am-a/business-corp/>.
- **INTIME** (Indiana Taxpayer Information Management Engine) is linked from DOR guidance for business tax filing.
- If you hire anyone, there may be state registration, withholding, unemployment insurance, and workers' compensation questions. Do not copy an employment package from the internet. Use official guidance and a professional.

### 4. Free business templates and education

- **SCORE resource library:** <https://www.score.org/resources/>. SCORE offers free business mentoring and a library of articles, templates, and tools. Templates are generic—check dates, assumptions, state-specific issues, and whether the licence allows your use.
- **SBA Business Guide:** <https://www.sba.gov/counseling/#business-guide>. Start here for business planning, launch, management, and funding basics.
- **SBA local assistance:** <https://www.sba.gov/counseling/local-assistance/>. SBA resource partners can provide free or low-cost counseling, but they do not replace your lawyer or CPA.
- **Indiana SBDC:** <https://isbdc.org/>. No-cost advising and training, including business planning and referrals.

## Template map for a tiny business

| Need | A reasonable starting point | What a template can't do |
|---|---|---|
| Form an Indiana LLC | INBiz official filing | Tell you whether an LLC is right for you or handle taxes/licenses |
| EIN | IRS EIN application | Choose your tax classification or answer state registration questions |
| Record customer TIN | IRS W-9 form | Decide whether you need one or what to do with it |
| Business plan / forecast | SBA and SCORE templates | Guarantee the assumptions are realistic |
| Client agreement / SOW | A starting point reviewed by an attorney | Fit your scope, liability, payment, IP, and Indiana law |
| NDA | Attorney-reviewed template | Tell you whether the protection is meaningful |
| Lease / office or studio | Commercial lease review by an attorney | Protect you from terms you didn't notice |
| Website privacy policy/terms | A draft that reflects your actual data practices, reviewed by counsel | Make you compliant by itself |
| Employment documents | Official agency guidance + an employment attorney | Cover worker classification, wage/hour, safety, and benefits |
| Wills, court filings, healthcare directives | Appropriate legal process and professional advice | Be handled casually with a monthly e-sign plan |

**No legal decision by checklist.** Use this map to know what to search for and what to ask, not as a green light.

## An operating agreement, specifically

An operating agreement is generally an internal document that describes how an LLC is run and how owners relate to it. The rules and practices around them vary, and the document isn't a generic form you can assume is right. Ask an Indiana business attorney:

- Do I need an operating agreement for my situation?
- What should it say about ownership, capital, distributions, decision-making, deadlocks, transfer, and dissolution?
- Does it need to be filed or kept internal?
- How should it interact with my tax elections and estate plan?
- What happens if I'm a single-member LLC?

Free templates can be a place to collect questions, not a final answer. This guide doesn't tell you whether you need one.

## How to vet any free template

- **Who published it?** Government? Bar association? Established nonprofit? Named law firm? Anonymous SEO site?
- **When was it updated?** A 2014 template can be worse than no template.
- **Is it state-specific?** Generic wording may conflict with Indiana rules.
- **What licence applies?** Can you use it commercially? Can you modify it? Are there attribution requirements?
- **What does it leave out?** Templates rarely know your scope, risks, compliance duties, or negotiation.
- **Is the layout hiding traps?** Check all pages, footnotes, and cross-references.
- **Does it contain placeholders you don't understand?** Don't sign or send it until you do.
- **Does it ask for sensitive data you shouldn't collect?** Limit data and secure storage.
- **Would you be comfortable reading it aloud to the other person?** If not, get help.

## What to bring to an attorney

- A one-page description of the business and the decision you're making.
- The draft document, clearly marked up with your questions.
- Names of the parties, relationship context, and timeline.
- Your budget and desired outcome.
- Relevant emails or prior agreements, redacted as appropriate for secure transmission.
- A list of questions ranked from "must understand" to "nice to know."

**Not every legal problem needs a full engagement.** Some attorneys offer short consultations, flat-fee reviews, or unbundled document review. Ask about cost and scope upfront. Your local bar association can help you find an appropriate lawyer. Free business advising from SCORE or the SBDC can help you prepare, but they are not your lawyer.

## AI helper: prepare for a legal conversation

```text
I am preparing questions for a licensed Indiana business attorney about
[describe the document or decision, without sensitive personal details].
My business is [one sentence]. I do not want you to draft a legal document
or tell me what the law is. Produce: 1) a one-page context summary I can
review, 2) eight specific questions to ask, 3) the documents I should
bring, 4) five terms I should ask them to explain in plain English, and
5) the facts I should verify before the meeting. Remind me to redact
sensitive information before sharing anything.
```

AI is a preparation aid. The attorney provides advice based on your facts and the actual law.
