# Invoicing: ask for money clearly, then follow up kindly

**The job:** Send a professional request for payment with unambiguous terms, make paying easy, and follow up without waiting three months and developing a stomachache.

> **Not legal or tax advice.** Contract terms, late fees, deposits, sales tax, and what happens when a client doesn't pay depend on your agreements and jurisdiction. Templates and scripts in this guide are educational starting points. For a high-value or disputed invoice, talk to an attorney or CPA.

## The free starting point

### Best all-free standalone: Zoho Invoice

[Zoho Invoice](https://www.zoho.com/invoice/) is free for what most one-person businesses need, with limits as of this writing: up to **two users, three projects, and 500 invoices per year**. It produces professional invoices, estimates, and payment links. It may display "Powered by Zoho" branding. Payment gateway fees are separate.

### Best if you already use Wave: Wave invoices

If you set up [Wave Starter](https://www.waveapps.com/pricing) for bookkeeping, use its invoicing instead of adding a second system. Fewer places for revenue to get lost. Wave's accounting and invoicing are free on Starter, with paid processing and optional add-ons.

**Pick one home for invoices.** Do not invoice from Wave for one client, Zoho for another, and a Canva PDF for a third. That is how records become archaeology.

## What a beginner-friendly invoice needs

Use plain fields and avoid legal-sounding filler you can't explain.

- Your business name and contact information
- Your customer's name and contact information
- A unique invoice number and issue date
- A clear description of what you delivered (or milestones)
- Quantity, rate, and total
- Any tax you're required to charge (ask a professional; don't guess)
- Payment terms: due date, accepted methods, and who pays processing fees, if consistent with your agreement
- Deposit or late-fee terms only if they were agreed in advance
- A link or instructions to pay
- A short, friendly note: what to do if something looks wrong
- The word **DRAFT** until you verify it

If you're selling physical goods, include shipping and return information where applicable. If you're selling digital goods or services, make sure the scope and delivery date are clear.

## Set it up without overthinking

1. **Create one branded template.** Business name, logo, one accent color, readable font. No decorative clutter.
2. **Set numbering.** Use a simple consistent scheme, such as `2026-001`, `2026-002`.
3. **Set payment terms.** Common options include due on receipt, Net 7, Net 14, or Net 30. Pick what you can actually enforce and state it on the invoice. For larger projects, ask about a deposit. Your contract controls; this is not legal advice.
4. **Connect a payment method only if needed.** Card payments take a percentage. Bank transfer options vary. Clearly distinguish what you receive from what the customer pays.
5. **Create a reusable follow-up schedule.** Put it on your calendar: due date, +3 days, +7 days, +14 days. Friendly persistence is not rude.
6. **Test with a $1 demo invoice to yourself** if the tool permits. Confirm the payment page, confirmation email, and receipt work.
7. **Download every paid invoice** and save it with the matching bookkeeping record.

## A simple follow-up ladder

Tone: warm, specific, and easy to act on. No passive-aggressive poems.

**Before due (2–3 days early):** "Hi [Name] — just a quick heads-up that invoice #2026-014 for [project] is due Friday. Here's the payment link if you need it. Thanks!"

**Due date:** "Hi [Name] — invoice #2026-014 is due today. I've attached a copy. If it's already scheduled, thank you—no need to reply. If something looks off, tell me and I'll take a look."

**3 days late:** "Hi [Name] — checking in on invoice #2026-014, now three days past due. Could you let me know the expected payment date? I'm happy to resend the link."

**7–14 days late:** "Hi [Name] — I still don't see payment for invoice #2026-014. Please send a payment date today, or let me know if there's a dispute so we can resolve it. If we agreed on a late fee, note it without inventing new terms."

**After 14 days:** pause, check your contract, and decide whether to escalate. If it's a significant amount or there's a dispute, get professional advice rather than sending a threatening template.

**Good boundaries:** stop additional work if your agreement allows it, keep everything in writing, and don't release final files or deliverables you aren't obligated to release. Check the actual contract—not a stranger's internet script.

## Deposits, late fees, and taxes

These are contractual and tax matters. The free-tool guide cannot decide them for you. Practical guardrails:

- **Put terms in the agreement, not only on the invoice.**
- **Don't invent late fees after a dispute.** If you want late fees, discuss the wording with a professional and include it upfront.
- **Check sales tax.** Services and products are treated differently in different places. Indiana businesses should start with the Indiana Department of Revenue and get advice for their specific offerings.
- **Use a CPA or tax pro for 1099 questions.** Whether you must issue or file forms is fact-specific.
- **Keep invoices and payments consistent.** If the invoice says $500 and the bank shows $487.25 after processing fees, record the fee as a fee—not as an invoice that was only partly paid.

## Free-plan watch-outs

- **Zoho Invoice is not a full accounting system.** Pair it with a bookkeeping workflow, or use Wave for both books and invoices. Don't assume your income is tracked just because you sent an invoice.
- **Zoho's free limits include 500 invoices/year and two users.** That's generous for a beginner, but confirm current terms on the official pricing page.
- **Payment links are not free money.** The payment processor charges a fee. Read the rate before enabling it.
- **Branding appears on free plans.** That is usually fine. Don't pay just to remove it until the business justifies it.
- **The customer's inbox is not your filing cabinet.** Download paid invoices and back them up.
- **Free plans can change.** Keep the export button on speed dial.

## AI helper: the follow-up draft

Never paste real customer names, bank details, or contract terms you don't own.

```text
You are a calm, friendly billing assistant for a one-person business.
Draft a payment follow-up email for this situation: [describe timeline,
amount, and prior contact in general terms, with no personal data]. Keep
it under 110 words. Be warm, specific, and firm. Include one clear next
step and a deadline. Do not threaten legal action, invent a late fee, or
claim legal rights. Give me two versions: one for a first reminder and one
for 14 days overdue.
```

Use it as a draft. Put the real facts in yourself. Verify the due date and contract terms.
