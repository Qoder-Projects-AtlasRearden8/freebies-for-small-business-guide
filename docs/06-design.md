# Design: look put-together without a design degree

**The job:** Make your business recognizable and readable across invoices, your website, social posts, and simple documents. Clarity is the design goal. Polish is a bonus.

> **Not legal advice.** Image licences, font licences, trademarks, and advertising rules matter. Use assets you have permission to use, don't copy another brand's distinctive identity, and verify licensing before publishing or selling merchandise.

## The free starting point: Canva Free

[Canva Free](https://www.canva.com/pricing/) is the easiest free design tool for a beginner. As of October 22, 2026, the free plan includes **5 GB of cloud storage** and up to **20 uses of Standard or Premium AI tools**, plus millions of free templates and assets. Premium elements are watermarked or require payment; you cannot just export them legally for free.

Good first uses:

- A simple logo lockup (your name in a chosen typeface, not a fake crest).
- One invoice header and one letterhead.
- A website hero graphic or open-graph card.
- Social profile images and a reusable announcement template.
- A one-page service or book sell sheet.
- A simple brand sheet for yourself.

### Understand the licensing

Canva's free content is generally available for commercial and noncommercial use under its content licence, but **premium content is different**, logos and brands can have extra restrictions, and licence terms can change. Read the current [Canva licensing explanation](https://www.canva.com/licensing-explained/) and [Content License Agreement](https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/) yourself.

Practical rules:

- Filter for free elements.
- Don't use a watermarked preview in a live project.
- Don't use Canva's stock or templates in a way the licence forbids (for example, some standalone asset resale uses).
- Don't upload someone else's copyrighted character, logo, or photo.
- Keep source links for any external asset you use.
- If you sell physical products with a design, check the licence for print-on-demand and merchandise.

## Build a tiny brand kit

You do not need a rebrand. You need consistency. Create a one-page note with:

- **Name and short description.** How the name is capitalized.
- **One primary color.** A deep green, navy, oxblood, or plain black. Dark text on light backgrounds is easiest.
- **One accent color.** Use it for links, buttons, and highlights—not every sentence.
- **Two typefaces at most.** One for headings, one for body. System fonts (Georgia, Palatino, Arial, Segoe UI) are free and render everywhere. If using web fonts, check the licence, load locally where possible, and don't depend on an external service for a privacy-first site.
- **One look for images.** Photos with the same warmth and light quality, or simple line illustrations. Avoid a collage of unrelated stock.
- **A clear voice.** Warm, direct, plain English; no fake urgency.
- **Contact details.** Business email, website, and city/state if appropriate. Don't put a private home address on public graphics.

### Starter palette recipe

Use a contrast checker before you publish. For normal text, aim for at least the WCAG AA contrast ratios (4.5:1 for small text; 3:1 for large text). Dark brown-green `#17342B` on warm cream `#F8F1E3` is an example of a high-contrast pairing. A bright orange on cream might look nice and still fail—check, don't vibe.

## Make the five assets you actually need

1. **A simple wordmark:** your business name set in one font, with good spacing. Skip the clip-art icon for now.
2. **A social profile image:** name readable at small size. Avoid tiny taglines.
3. **A link-preview image:** 1200 × 630 px usually works for sharing. Include your name and a short line.
4. **An invoice header:** logo or name, email, website. Use the same header on quotes.
5. **One social/announcement template:** duplicate it, change the text, keep the layout.

Then **stop**. A brand is not a 60-page PDF. It's recognition plus trust.

## Design rules that make a beginner look experienced

- **Hierarchy:** one big headline, one supporting line, one call to action. If everything is loud, nothing is.
- **Whitespace:** let things breathe. Margins are not wasted space.
- **Alignment:** pick a left edge and stick to it. Canva's alignment guides are your friend.
- **Contrast:** dark text on light backgrounds, or light text on genuinely dark backgrounds. Don't use yellow text on white.
- **Small text:** don't go below sensible body sizes (roughly 16px on the web). On print, no 5-point footnotes.
- **Images:** one strong image beats five decorative ones. Provide alt text on the web.
- **Consistency:** same colors, same two fonts, same logo placement.
- **Restraint with AI images:** don't create fake people who look like your customers or fake product photos. Disclose material use where appropriate. Keep human judgment.

## File organization that saves future-you

Create `Brand/` with:

```text
Brand/
  brand-sheet.md
  logo/
    wordmark-light.png
    wordmark-dark.png
    favicon.svg
  templates/
    invoice-header
    social-square
    link-preview
  licenses/
    asset-sources.md
```

Export PNG for everyday sharing, SVG or PDF for print-quality logos when available. Keep editable Canva links and a local exported copy. If your Canva account goes away, you should still have the assets you need.

## Accessible by default

- Use real text instead of text baked into images when possible.
- Keep body copy at a comfortable size and line length.
- Check contrast (a free contrast checker is enough).
- Add captions/transcripts to video.
- Don't rely on color alone to communicate (for example, red/green only).
- Add alt text for informative images: describe the purpose, not every pixel.
- Make buttons and links look clickable.
- Test your site with the biggest text setting and keyboard-only navigation.

## AI helper: make a design brief, not a logo dump

```text
Create a one-page design brief for my small business. Ask me five short
questions about my audience, the feeling I want, references I like, and
where the design will be used. Then propose: one primary color, one accent
color, two system-font choices, five layout rules, and a list of five
assets I actually need. Do not invent a logo concept or claim the colors
pass accessibility—tell me to test contrast with a checker. Keep the tone
warm and plain.
```

Use AI for structure, editing, and alternatives. Don't ask it to imitate a living artist's style or reproduce a trademark.

## Free-plan watch-outs

- **Premium templates and images can trap you at download.** Canva Free may let you edit with premium elements but require payment or a watermarked export. Filter to free.
- **AI usage is limited on the free plan.** As of this check, up to 20 uses. Don't plan a 30-asset AI campaign on it.
- **Cloud storage is not a backup.** Export and back up important assets.
- **Canva is not a trademark search.** A name that looks good can still infringe someone else's rights.
- **Free fonts on one site may cost money on another.** Check the licence for web, print, merchandise, and embedding.
