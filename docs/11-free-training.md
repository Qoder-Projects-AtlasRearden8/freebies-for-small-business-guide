# Free training & templates: learn the skill, not just collect the certificate

**The job:** Build the specific skills your business actually needs—bookkeeping basics, marketing, a little web work, AI literacy—without buying a $499 course you never finish. Templates are part of the same job: they give you a decent starting point so you can spend your energy on the parts that are uniquely yours.

> **This is education, not legal, tax, or financial advice.** A course, certificate, or template does not make you a lawyer, accountant, or licensed professional. Training quality and free offers change. Check the provider's official page before you invest time. Never pay an unsolicited "training" provider that guarantees income or government approvals.

## The learning rule that saves the most time

> **Learn one skill that removes a current bottleneck, practice it on your real business, then stop.**

Collecting courses feels productive. Applying one lesson to a real invoice, a real webpage, or a real customer email is what changes the business. A 45-minute session with a blank page beats a 40-hour video library you bookmark and avoid.

## Free training worth knowing

### Government and nonprofit training

- **SBA Learning Platform** — free, self-paced courses on starting, planning, marketing, finance, and managing a business: <https://learning.sba.gov/>.
- **SCORE** — free mentoring plus free webinars, courses, and a large resource library: <https://www.score.org/> and <https://www.score.org/resources/>.
- **Indiana SBDC** — no-cost one-on-one advising and training for Indiana businesses: <https://isbdc.org/>.
- **IRS Small Business and Self-Employed Tax Center** — official guides, forms, and recordkeeping basics: <https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed>.
- **FTC Business Guidance** — plain-language consumer-protection and advertising rules: <https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance>.
- **DigitalLearn** — free, beginner-friendly computer and internet basics from the Public Library Association: <https://www.digitallearn.org/>.
- **Your local public library** — many libraries offer free access to online course platforms, research databases, and in-person tech help. Ask your branch what your card includes; offerings vary.

## Free templates that are safe to start with

| Need | Free starting point | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Business plan outline | SBA Business Guide and SCORE templates | Update assumptions; don't copy someone else's market data |
| Cash-flow forecast | SCORE or SBA template, or a simple spreadsheet | Make the formulas visible and test them |
| Client agreement or NDA | A template reviewed by an attorney (see [Legal templates](10-legal-templates.md)) | Scope, payment, liability, intellectual property, Indiana law |
| Invoice | Zoho Invoice Free or Wave | Tax lines, payment terms, numbering |
| Social graphics | Canva Free templates | Filter to free elements; check licences |
| Link-preview image | Canva Free, 1200 × 630 px | Readable text at small size |
| Documents and letters | Microsoft Create templates | Remove placeholder text and tracked changes |
| Spreadsheet CRM | A sheet you build with the columns in [CRM](03-crm.md) | Exportability and privacy |
| Email signup form | MailerLite or Brevo template | Consent language, footer address, unsubscribe |
| Book-publishing checklist | KDP Help and your own outline | Royalties, file formats, tax interview, metadata |

**Templates are not strategy.** A business plan template with optimistic numbers is still optimistic. A contract template that doesn't match your scope is still a contract. Use templates to organize thinking, not to replace it.

## A four-week learning plan you can actually finish

One 45-minute session per week. Pick a real task, not a certificate.

**Week 1 — Money literacy (45 min).**
Watch one SBA or SCORE session on cash flow. Then open your own numbers and write down three questions. Book a free SBDC or SCORE appointment to ask them.

**Week 2 — Marketing that fits you (45 min).**
Take one HubSpot Academy or Google Skillshop lesson. Write one useful email or one service page using what you learned. Publish it.

**Week 3 — Your website or design (45 min).**
Use one Canva Design School lesson to fix one real asset: homepage header, invoice header, or social profile. Export it and use it.

**Week 4 — AI with boundaries (45 min).**
Read [AI helpers](13-ai-helpers.md) and set up one assistant for low-risk drafting. Write your redaction rules. Test one prompt on a real, non-sensitive task.

At the end, write one sentence: "I learned ___ and used it to ___." If you can't fill in the second blank, the course was entertainment, not training.

## How to vet free training

- **Who made it?** Official agency, established nonprofit, recognized company, or anonymous funnel?
- **When was it updated?** Tax, platform, and marketing advice goes stale quickly.
- **Is there a real exercise?** Watching is not the same as doing.
- **What is the upsell?** A free lesson that ends in a $2,000 coaching pitch is an advertisement, not a course.
- **Does it promise income or approvals?** "Guaranteed" is a warning sign, not a feature.
- **Does the certificate mean anything?** Usually it documents attendance, not professional licensure. Don't put a vendor certificate in a bio as if it were a degree or credential.
- **Is your data safe?** Don't upload customer records, health information, or financial account numbers to an unknown learning platform.
- **Can you use the template commercially?** Read the licence. "Free" often means personal use only.

## AI helper: turn a course into practice

```text
I have 45 minutes to learn [skill] for my small business. My current
bottleneck is [describe the problem in general terms]. Build a practice-
first session: 10 minutes of core concepts, 25 minutes of a real exercise
using a made-up example, and 10 minutes of reflection. Give me the
exercise steps, a checklist to judge my own work, and three questions to
ask a free SBDC or SCORE advisor. Do not invent statistics or links.
Do not promise income results. This is learning help, not legal, tax, or
financial advice.
```

Use AI to structure practice. Then verify any factual claim against the official source.
