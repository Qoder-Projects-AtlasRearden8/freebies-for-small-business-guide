# Free help in Indiana & the US: real people, no sales pitch

**The job:** Know where to get qualified, free or low-cost guidance before you guess, overpay, or fall for a scam. The best free resource is not a PDF—it's a person who has seen your situation before and will tell you what to do next.

> **This chapter is education, not legal, tax, or financial advice.** Free advisors can explain programs and offer general guidance; they are not your attorney, CPA, or financial advisor, and they cannot guarantee a loan, grant, contract, or approval. Verify eligibility and deadlines with the official agency. Never pay an unsolicited caller who says they can "unlock" a government grant.

## Who to contact first

For most Indiana one-person businesses, the shortest path is:

1. **Indiana SBDC** for no-cost business advising and a local point of contact: <https://isbdc.org/>.
2. **SCORE** for a free mentor and free workshops, especially if you want a long-term sounding board: <https://www.score.org/>.
3. **SBA local assistance** to find the resource partner that fits your situation: <https://www.sba.gov/counseling/local-assistance/>.
4. **The official agency** for filings, taxes, and licenses—INBiz, the Indiana Department of Revenue, or the IRS. Free advice does not replace an official filing.

Free does not mean low quality. SCORE volunteers are often retired executives. SBDC advisors work with small businesses every day. Come prepared and you will get more than a generic brochure.

> **A note on "free" advising:** SCORE and SBDC advising are designed to be no-cost, but always ask before a meeting whether any service has a fee. Legitimate organizations do not demand a payment to "hold" your free appointment or "activate" a grant.

## What free help can and cannot do

**A good advisor can:**
- Explain how a program, form, or process works in plain English.
- Review your plan or numbers and point out gaps.
- Help you prepare questions for an attorney, CPA, or lender.
- Connect you with local programs, workshops, and peers.
- Tell you when a paid professional is genuinely worth it.

**An advisor cannot:**
- Give you legal advice or sign off on your contract.
- Prepare or sign your tax return.
- Approve a loan, grant, or government contract.
- Guarantee that a business idea will work.
- Make a decision that is yours to make.

If someone offers a guarantee, treats a free service as a sales funnel, or asks for sensitive information before you have verified who they are, stop and verify through the official website.

## How to get real value from a 30-minute appointment

**Before the meeting, write a one-page brief:**
- What the business does, in one sentence.
- Who it serves and how it makes money.
- The specific decision or bottleneck you need help with.
- What you have already tried.
- The three questions you most need answered.
- Your timeline and rough budget.

**Bring or have ready:** your business name and entity type, your website or draft, a simple revenue/expense picture, and any relevant document with sensitive details redacted. Do not send your Social Security number, EIN, bank credentials, or customer health information by ordinary email.

**During the meeting:**
- Ask, "What would you do next if this were your business?"
- Ask, "What am I not asking that I should be?"
- Write down the advice and the source.
- Ask whether they can refer you to a specialist.

**After the meeting:** send a two-line thank-you that summarizes the next step. Put the follow-up on your calendar.

## A short outreach email

```text
Subject: Request for a free advising session — [business name]

Hi [organization],

I'm the owner of [business name], a [one-sentence description] in
[city], Indiana. I'm working on [specific decision or problem] and would
like a 30-minute advising session.

Current situation: [2–3 sentences, no sensitive data].
What I've tried: [1–2 sentences].
My three questions:
1. [question]
2. [question]
3. [question]

I'm available [two time windows]. I understand this is general business
guidance, not legal or tax advice. Thank you.

[Name]
[Business website or phone]
```

Keep it under 200 words. A clear, specific request gets a faster reply than "I want to start a business, please advise."

## Scam protection, in plain English

Government resources are free to access. Anyone charging you to "apply for an EIN," "file your LLC," or "claim a free grant" is selling a service or running a scam.

**Red flags:**
- An unsolicited call, text, or DM saying you qualify for a "free government grant."
- A fee before you can see the grant, loan, or program details.
- A website that looks like the SBA or INBiz but has a different URL.
- Pressure to pay today, or a "deadline" that disappears tomorrow.
- A request for your SSN, EIN, bank login, or a copy of your ID before you have verified the organization.
- A "mentor" who guarantees approvals, asks you to buy gift cards, or moves the conversation to a private messaging app.
- Paid "certification" or "registration" that is not required by any agency.

**Safer habits:**
- Type the official address yourself: `sba.gov`, `irs.gov`, `inbiz.in.gov`, `in.gov`.
- Search the organization's name plus "scam" before paying anyone.
- Use a separate business email and a password manager.
- Ask an advisor you met through the SBDC or SCORE to review an offer before you sign.
- Report suspected fraud at <https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/> and, for internet crime, <https://www.ic3.gov/>.

## Specialized free help

- **Veterans and military spouses:** SBA Veterans Business Outreach Centers.
- **Women entrepreneurs:** SBA Women's Business Centers.
- **Minority-owned businesses:** MBDA Business Centers.
- **Government contracting:** APEX Accelerators.
- **Exporting:** SBA and the U.S. Commercial Service offer counseling and market information; start with the SBA local assistance page.
- **Agriculture, childcare, and other regulated sectors:** ask the SBDC for the agency that governs your industry before relying on general advice.

Eligibility rules vary. A center that is not the right fit will usually refer you; that is still a useful call.

```text

SBA resource partner]. My business is [one sentence]. The decision I need
help with is [describe, without sensitive data]. Produce: 1) a one-page
```
