## The freebie scorecard

Before you build a habit around a free tool, score it on these ten points. Any "no" is a conversation with yourself, not automatically a dealbreaker.

1. **Real free tier:** Can you do the core job forever, or is this a short trial?
2. **Core-job completeness:** Does the free plan require a paid add-on for the thing you actually need?
3. **Export:** Can you get your data out in a usable format? Have you tested it?
4. **Limits:** Do you know the contact, document, storage, send, or usage caps?
5. **Commercial use:** Does the licence allow business use, client work, or resale? Check the actual licence.
6. **Privacy and data use:** What does the provider collect, and does it train on your data? Is the tool appropriate for the information you're putting in it?
7. **Security:** Two-factor authentication, access controls, and a credible security page.
8. **Support:** Can you get help without paying, and is there a real documentation page?
9. **Longevity:** Is the provider established, and do you have a replacement plan if the free tier changes?
10. **Total cost:** Domain, payment processing, storage, exports, upsells, and your time. "Free" is not the same as "no cost."

**The exit test:** Before you store anything important, find the export button, export a sample, and open the file. If you cannot leave, do not move in.

## Scam and trap patterns

Free tools and government programs attract copycats. These patterns are common:

- **"Free government grant" calls or texts.** SBA does not call you out of the blue to award a grant. Grants are specific, competitive, and documented on official sites. Never pay a fee to "release" a grant.
- **Paid EIN or LLC filing services.** You can apply for an EIN free at the IRS and file formation documents at INBiz. Third-party services can be legitimate, but cold calls and look-alike sites are not the place to start.
- **Look-alike government domains.** A site with an SBA logo and a slightly wrong URL is not the SBA. Type `sba.gov`, `irs.gov`, `inbiz.in.gov`, or `in.gov` yourself.
- **Free trials with automatic renewal.** Put a cancellation reminder on your calendar before the trial ends. Use a virtual card or a payment method with a low limit if available.
- **"Lifetime" deals with no export.** A lifetime price on a tool that holds your only copy of customer data is a hostage situation, not a bargain.
- **Fake mentors and guaranteed approvals.** A legitimate free advisor does not demand payment, gift cards, crypto, or your bank login. Verify the organization through the SBDC, SCORE, or SBA.
- **"Free" templates you cannot use commercially.** Read the licence. Personal-use-only templates are not a business foundation.
- **AI tools that ask for credentials.** Never paste passwords, API keys, or recovery codes into a chat. A legitimate AI product does not need them to draft an email.
- **Unsolicited tax or bookkeeping help.** Verify the preparer's credentials and the official filing channel. A confident stranger is not a CPA.

**Report fraud:** <https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/> and <https://www.ic3.gov/>. Report suspected impersonation to the agency being imitated.

## How to re-check a freebie in 10 minutes

Once a quarter, for each tool you depend on:

1. Open the official pricing page and note today's date.
2. Check the limits against your current usage.
3. Read the data-use and training settings.
4. Test an export and open the file.
5. Check whether the plan still covers the core job.
6. Look for a replacement that can import your data.
7. Update your own notes—not just this guide.

If you adapt this guide for your audience, keep the checked date visible and re-verify the links before you publish. A confidently outdated freebie list is worse than no list.
