# Forms: ask less, get better answers

**The job:** Collect the information you need for a quote request, intake, feedback survey, or application—without becoming a data hoarder. A good form is short, accessible, and clear about where the answers go.

> **Not legal or tax advice.** Privacy, consent, data-retention, healthcare, children's data, and payment-card rules are specialized. When in doubt, collect less and get professional guidance. Never ask for Social Security numbers, passwords, full card numbers, or medical details through a generic form.

## The free starting point

### Tally Free

[Tally](https://tally.so/pricing) offers **unlimited forms and submissions** on its free plan, with features such as logic, integrations, and payments, and Tally branding. It's a clean, beginner-friendly form builder and often the best fit for a small business that needs a professional-looking intake form for free.

- **Free file uploads:** individual files up to **10 MB** on the free plan as of this writing. Check current storage and submission terms.
- **Perks:** conditional logic, answers in a spreadsheet-like view, integrations, and a simple editor.
- **Catch:** free-plan branding, and advanced features or higher limits may require a paid plan.

### Google Forms Free

[Google Forms](https://forms.google.com/) is free with a Google account. It's basic, familiar, and stores responses in Google Sheets or Drive. It's a good choice if you already live in Google Workspace-adjacent tools and need a simple questionnaire.

- **Strengths:** quick setup, spreadsheet responses, simple sharing.
- **Weaknesses:** less control over advanced logic and design; data lives in a third-party ecosystem; account security matters.
- **Watch:** consumer Gmail accounts and business accounts have different data/administration implications. Don't put sensitive records in a personal form.
- **Storage limits:** Google's help documentation lists a **1 GB total storage limit** and a **500,000 responses per form** limit for Forms starting September 2026. File uploads can consume that storage quickly. Check the current limit before using Forms as a file-intake system.

**Pick one.** Don't collect the same answers in two forms and then manage two incompatible spreadsheets.

## What belongs in a beginner form

Use exact, neutral labels. Make the required minimum obvious. Examples:

**For a quote request**
- Name and preferred contact method
- Email
- What do you need help with? (long answer)
- Desired deadline and budget range (optional, if appropriate)
- How did you find me?
- Consent checkbox: "OK to contact me about this request."

**For a workshop interest list**
- Name (optional)
- Email
- One question you want answered
- Consent checkbox with a link to how you'll use the email

**For feedback**
- One rating, one open question, and one optional contact field
- Thank-you page with next steps

Do not ask for a birthdate, home address, income, diagnosis, or ID number unless there is a specific, legitimate business reason and a safe system. A form is not a secure vault.

## Build a form that gets completed

1. **Write the decision first.** "What will I do with this response?" If there's no action, remove the question.
2. **Name the form in plain language.** "Project request" beats "Inquiry Intake v3."
3. **Use one page for a short form.** Long forms need sections; short forms need respect.
4. **Use clear field labels.** Labels above fields, not placeholder-only text that disappears.
5. **Mark optional fields as optional.** Don't make everything required.
6. **Add help text** for confusing questions. "Budget range helps me scope this" is friendlier than an unlabelled dropdown.
7. **Explain privacy.** Where will responses go? Who sees them? How long will you keep them?
8. **Add a confirmation message.** Tell people what happens next and when to expect a reply.
9. **Test accessibility.** Keyboard navigation, visible focus, error messages, and sufficient contrast.
10. **Test the notification and export.** Submit a fake entry, receive the email, find the row, export a CSV.

## Data hygiene for one-person businesses

- **Use a business account** with two-factor authentication.
- **Limit access.** Don't share the admin login.
- **Review responses on a schedule.** Turn off collection when the campaign ends.
- **Export and archive** what you need for your records, then consider deleting unnecessary form submissions.
- **Don't use a form for e-signatures.** A typed "I agree" field in a general form isn't a complete signing workflow. Use a dedicated e-signature tool and get legal advice for important agreements.
- **Avoid healthcare data.** If you educate healthcare workers, keep educational feedback separate from patient information. Never collect PHI through a free form.
- **Be careful with file uploads.** Accept only the formats you need, set size expectations, and never allow executable files.
- **Watch third-party integrations.** Every connected app is another place data can leak.
- **Spam happens.** Add a simple challenge or accept that you'll delete junk. Don't collect extra personal data just to "verify everyone."

## Free-plan watch-outs

- **"Unlimited submissions" does not mean unlimited uploads or features.** File sizes, storage, and advanced capabilities vary.
- **Free branding appears.** That can be a trust signal for a small business, not an emergency.
- **Personal Google accounts lack business-grade admin controls.** Fine for low-risk; not for sensitive data.
- **Deleting a form may not immediately delete every response copy.** Check the help docs and maintain your own retention plan.
- **Third-party form tools process data on your behalf.** Read the privacy policy if you collect customer data.

## AI helper: turn a messy list of questions into a decent short form

```text
I need a form for [purpose]. Here are my rough questions: [list]. Rewrite
them as clear, accessible labels, remove anything that is not necessary
for this purpose, and mark optional fields. Flag any request for sensitive
personal data and suggest a safer alternative. Produce a short consent
sentence, a confirmation message, and a one-page internal data-retention
note. Do not claim that this makes me compliant with any privacy law.
```

Then delete at least one question. That's not an AI instruction; it's a form-design rule.
