# Accounting: know the numbers without becoming an accountant

**The job:** Keep a clean, understandable record of money that came in, money that went out, and what still belongs to whom. You are not trying to produce a tax return by yourself. You are trying to avoid a shoebox in April.

> **Not tax, accounting, or financial advice.** Expense treatment, estimated taxes, payroll, sales tax, inventory, home offices, and multi-state issues are fact-specific. Use a CPA or enrolled agent for decisions. Free tools help you organize; they don't replace professional review.

## The free starting point: Wave Starter

[Wave](https://www.waveapps.com/) offers a free Starter plan in the US and Canada that includes core accounting and unlimited invoicing. Paid add-ons and card processing exist; you do not need them to start bookkeeping.

Why it's the recommendation for a very small US business:

- It's one place for income, expenses, and invoices.
- It works on a phone and in a browser.
- The free plan is usable rather than a short trial.
- It can connect to a bank account, which reduces manual entry.

**Alternative for a non-US business:** Wave's availability varies. Check local free options and local accounting requirements. Do not pretend a US tool handles another country's tax rules.

**Alternative for a spreadsheet lover:** A simple spreadsheet is honest and infinitely exportable. It is not the best choice if you will forget to update it or need bank connections. If you use a spreadsheet, log every transaction, keep receipts, reconcile monthly, and export a copy.

## What "good bookkeeping" actually looks like

At minimum, every transaction should answer:

1. **Date** — when it happened.
2. **Description** — what it was.
3. **Amount** — how much.
4. **Account/category** — what kind of money it was.
5. **Receipt or evidence** — a document that supports it.
6. **Business purpose** — a short note for anything unclear.

For a one-person business, a monthly rhythm matters more than a fancy chart of accounts. A clean modest set of categories beats 87 micro-categories you can't explain.

## Set up Wave in about 45 minutes

1. **Create the free account.** Choose the Starter plan. Dismiss paid add-ons unless you already know you need them.
2. **Add the business details.** Legal name, address, currency, and logo if you have one. Use your business email.
3. **Connect the business account.** Read the permissions screen. Wave uses a third-party connection provider; understand what it can see. If you're not ready, skip the connection and add transactions manually this week.
4. **Set a simple chart of accounts.** Start with broad buckets such as sales, contract income, software, supplies, advertising, transaction fees, professional services, and owner draws/contributions. If you're unsure, leave something labeled "Ask my accountant" rather than guessing.
5. **Import only the current year.** Don't recreate six years of history unless a professional says to. Duplicate transactions create duplicate problems.
6. **Categorize 10 transactions.** Learn where the review screen lives. Don't aim for perfection; aim for a repeatable monthly pass.
7. **Attach your first receipts.** Save a PDF or image to your `Money/2026` folder. Receipt scanning may be a paid add-on; a phone photo plus a consistent folder is still useful.
8. **Create a customer and one invoice.** You can learn invoicing in the next chapter.
9. **Label owner money correctly.** If you take money out of the business, don't call it a mysterious expense. Ask a professional how owner draws/contributions should be recorded for your tax classification.
10. **Export a test copy.** If you can't find the export screen, you're not ready to trust the tool. Make notes.

## The 25-minute monthly money date

Put it on the calendar. Bring a drink. Do the same steps in the same order.

**Step 1 — Reconcile (8 min).** Match the bank transactions in Wave to reality. Investigate duplicates and missing transactions. The goal is that Wave agrees with the bank.

**Step 2 — Categorize and annotate (8 min).** Clear the review queue. For anything ambiguous, write a question instead of inventing an answer. Save it on a running "ask my accountant" list.

**Step 3 — Check invoices (4 min).** Look at unpaid invoices and send a friendly reminder. Record any payment fees. Don't count money as received until it's actually received.

**Step 4 — Close the month (3 min).** Export a CSV of transactions and a simple profit-and-loss report. Download any new invoices and receipts into the monthly folder. Write a one-line summary: "October: two invoices paid, one late, $X in business software."

**Step 5 — Make one decision (2 min).** Choose one small improvement for next month: ask for a deposit, raise a price, cancel an unused tool, or book help.

## A note about receipts

Sophisticated receipt scanning is convenient, but not required on day one. What matters:

- Save receipts the same week.
- Name files with date, vendor, and amount.
- Keep them in a private folder with backups.
- Match receipts to the transaction list.
- Ask a professional how long to keep records for your situation.

Wave's receipt-scanning feature may require a paid add-on. Check the current pricing page. Also check whether a mobile photo stored in your own folder is enough for your workflow; don't pay for scanning just to create the same result.

## Free-plan watch-outs

- **Card payments and bank connections are not the same as accounting.** Payment processing fees are separate from the free bookkeeping plan.
- **Wave's "free" can include upsells.** The account is free; payroll, payments, receipts, or other modules may cost money. You can say no.
- **No tax filing included.** Wave may offer tax products in some regions, but you remain responsible for filing. A free tool is not a tax preparer.
- **Data export matters.** Test the export now, not during an emergency.
- **Don't mix personal and business spending if you can avoid it.** A clean account saves hours. Ask your bank about fee-free business options.
- **AI can organize; it cannot sign off.** Never ask a chatbot to determine your tax deduction without verifying with a professional.

## AI helper: the monthly close buddy

Use a free AI assistant only after removing names, account numbers, and other sensitive details.

```text
Act as a careful bookkeeping assistant, not a CPA. I will paste
anonymized descriptions of this month's transactions. Sort them into my
existing categories, list anything ambiguous as a question rather than a
guess, and create a 20-minute month-end checklist. Do not advise me on
tax deductions, filing positions, or whether an expense is deductible.
Do not invent amounts or vendors. End with three questions to ask my
accountant.
```

**Good uses of AI here:** explain a confusing report in plain English, draft a checklist, summarize a month you describe, generate a list of questions.

**Bad uses:** paste raw bank statements, ask it to file taxes, ask it to decide if something is deductible, trust its math without checking.
